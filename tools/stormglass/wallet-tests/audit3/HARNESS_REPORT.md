## Page defects found by the harness audit (for the page agent; index.html not edited here)
1. **The opBegin wrong-chain stop had no test of its own (M5 survived).** Trigger: the wallet is on Base (0x2105), the visitor presses WITNESS (or CLAIM), and the wallet refuses the switch (4001). On the real page today opBegin returns null and the visitor sees "Your wallet is on another network. Switch it to Ethereum mainnet. Nothing was sent.". The wallet is asked nothing more (a1 now shows estimateGas 0, sendTransaction 0). With the stop removed, the visitor sees "Your witness payment: confirm in your wallet", the wallet is asked `eth_estimateGas` on Base, and then opCheck says "Your wallet moved to another network. Nothing was sent.". That is a false prompt plus a misleading reason, and opCheck is the only guard left: with both removed the payment goes out on Base. This is not a defect in the current page. It was a coverage gap, now closed by a1 (a4e8405). No page change is needed.
2. **The "Cancelled" toast after refusing the network switch in COLLECT. It is present on the unmutated page, not caused by M5.** Trigger: wallet on Base, COLLECT, the dialog shows "[!!] YOUR WALLET IS ON ANOTHER NETWORK" with SWITCH TO ETHEREUM. The visitor taps it and refuses the switch in the wallet (code 4001). `switchChain()` (index.html ~1046) catches the error and toasts `errMsg(e)`, which for 4001 is the generic "Cancelled in your wallet. Nothing was sent." (errMsg ~1015). The dialog correctly stays on the wrong-network view. The header chain bar (`#nx-chainBar` -> switchChain) does the same. What the visitor sees reads like a cancelled purchase, but what they refused was the network switch. A suggested wording: "You kept your wallet on another network. Nothing was sent. Switch to Ethereum mainnet to collect." (or "Network switch cancelled in your wallet…"). No harness assertion was added because the page has no stated wording to test against. a2 still checks only that nothing is sent.

## Harness gaps fixed (summary)
Duplicate ids passing; a silent exit 0 on browser loss; inner-context page errors ignored; contexts leaked by inner boots and by failed boots; WebSockets (the real WalletConnect relay) not intercepted; service workers not blocked; outside traffic recorded but never reported; no list of what ran; run.sh running against a foreign server when the port was taken; g and m5 not asserting their barrier; a1 not asserting that the wallet was left alone after a refused switch (M5).
